CRITICAL DISTINCTION

A technical problem and a losing trading strategy are not the same thing. Fixing a technical issue does not guarantee profitable trading.

9 Common Reasons Why Your Trading Bot Is Not Working

1

Your Internet Connection Is Unstable

Many trading tools require a stable internet connection to communicate with a trading platform or external service. If your connection drops, the bot may lose communication, fail to update information, or stop responding.

How to check:
  • Open another website to see whether your internet is working.
  • Switch between Wi-Fi and another reliable connection if available.
  • Check whether your browser or platform displays a connection warning.
  • Review the bot's status or error messages.

Avoid repeatedly restarting the bot without understanding whether it is still running. First, confirm its actual status through the relevant platform.

2

The Trading Platform or Session Has a Problem

Your bot may depend on an active trading platform session. If you have been logged out, your session has expired, or the platform is experiencing a technical issue, the bot may be unable to continue.

How to check:
  • Open the official trading platform directly.
  • Confirm that you can access your account.
  • Check for platform warnings or scheduled maintenance announcements.
  • If necessary, sign in again through the official website.
  • Reconnect the bot only after confirming that the previous session has ended safely.
3

Your Bot Configuration Is Incorrect

A bot may start successfully but fail to execute trades because its configuration is incomplete or incorrect. For example, a strategy might require a particular market, trade duration, stake amount, or set of conditions.

How to check:
  • Review the selected market or trading instrument.
  • Confirm that required inputs have valid values.
  • Check the configured stake and trade conditions.
  • Look for missing blocks, invalid values, or configuration warnings.
  • Compare your current settings with the strategy you intended to run.

Make one change at a time and test it in a demo environment where possible.

4

Your Strategy's Conditions Have Not Been Met

Sometimes a bot is working correctly but does not open a trade. This can happen when the conditions in its strategy have not been satisfied (e.g. waiting for a price level, indicator condition, or specific market movement).

What you should do:
  • Review the strategy's entry conditions.
  • Check whether the required market conditions have occurred.
  • Inspect available logs or status messages.
  • Test the strategy using historical data or a demo account if supported.

A bot that does not open a trade is not necessarily broken. Avoid weakening entry rules just to force trades.

5

The Selected Market or Trading Product Is Unsupported

Not every trading bot supports every market, trading product, or platform. A bot designed for one environment may not work with another.

How to check:
  • Read the bot provider's compatibility documentation.
  • Confirm that your intended platform is supported.
  • Verify that the selected market and contract type are available.
  • Check whether the bot requires a particular platform version.

If using Deriv, start with official Deriv trading platforms > documentation.

6

Your Account Has a Permission or Limit Issue

Some bots need specific permissions or an authorised connection. Issues include insufficient balance for the configured stake, trade requests exceeding limits, or expired token authorisation.

How to check:
  • Read the complete error message.
  • Confirm that the trading feature is available on your account.
  • Check available balance and the configured stake.
  • Review applicable trading limits or account restrictions.
  • Reauthorise the connection only through the official platform.

Never share passwords or one-time verification codes with anyone claiming they can repair your bot.

7

An API or External Integration Has Failed

Some automated trading tools connect via an API or webhook integration. If the connection token expires or a request fails, the bot stops receiving market feeds or sending order instructions.

Possible troubleshooting steps:
  • Check the provider's official status page or support notice.
  • Review connection and authentication error messages.
  • Confirm that the integration is supported and configured correctly.
  • Renew authorization through the official process if necessary.
8

Your Browser, Software, or Bot Version Has a Problem

An outdated browser, a software bug, incompatible extension settings, or a recent update can interfere with trading tools.

Try these steps:
  • Reload the page after confirming no active operation is interrupted.
  • Check whether your browser or bot software needs an update.
  • Test in a supported browser if recommended by the provider.
  • Record the exact error message before contacting technical support.
9

The Bot Has Stopped After an Error or Reached a Configured Limit

Some tools stop after encountering an unhandled exception or reaching a configured condition (e.g. daily trade limit, max loss limit, consecutive loss threshold).

What to do:
  • Check whether the bot is still running.
  • Read its latest status and error messages.
  • Review configured stopping conditions and limits.
  • Confirm whether any trade remains open.
  • Check the platform's trade history before attempting another run.

Do not remove risk limits simply to keep a bot running. Those settings are important safeguards.

A Step-by-Step Trading Bot Troubleshooting Checklist

Use this checklist before making major changes to your bot:

Confirm that your internet connection is stable.
Open the official trading platform and check your session.
Read the full error message instead of guessing.
Review your bot configuration and trading conditions.
Verify market and platform compatibility.
Check account permissions, available balance, and applicable limits.
Review the integration status and software version.
Confirm whether any trades are open or pending.
Test changes in a demo environment where available.
Contact the official provider if the issue remains unresolved.
SAFETY TIP:

If you cannot confirm whether the bot is still active, avoid launching another instance. Two running instances may create duplicate trades or unexpected exposure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my trading bot not opening trades?

Your bot may be waiting for its strategy conditions to be met. It could also have incorrect settings, an unsupported market, an account restriction, or a connection problem. Check its status and logs before changing the strategy.

Why does my trading bot keep disconnecting?

Possible causes include an unstable internet connection, an expired session, a provider-side issue, or a failed integration. Check official platform and provider information to narrow down the cause.

Why is my Deriv Bot not working?

Check that you can access the official platform, review the bot's configuration, confirm that its conditions are valid, and read any displayed errors. For platform-specific guidance, visit the Deriv Help Centre >.

Should I restart my trading bot after an error?

Not immediately. First, check whether it has active trades, identify the error, and confirm that restarting will not create duplicate trades or unwanted exposure.

Can a trading bot stop working because it is losing money?

A losing strategy and a technical failure are different problems. A bot can execute its instructions correctly and still lose money. Review its technical status separately from its trading performance.

What should I do if I cannot fix my trading bot?

Save the error message, note when the problem started, and contact the official platform or bot provider through its verified support channel. Never send passwords or verification codes as part of a support request.

Final Thoughts

When a trading bot stops working, identify the cause before changing settings or restarting it. Check the connection, platform session, configuration, compatibility, account permissions, and error messages in a structured way.

Most importantly, remember that a functioning bot is not necessarily a profitable bot. Technical reliability, strategy evaluation, and risk management must be considered separately. Use trusted documentation, test changes carefully, and never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
