Decision Framework

The right choice depends on your experience, trading platform, strategy, budget, and risk tolerance. Instead of choosing a bot because of impressive advertisements or profit claims, take time to understand how it works and what it can actually do. In this guide, you will learn seven important things to check before choosing a trading bot.

What Is a Trading Bot?

A trading bot is software designed to perform certain trading-related tasks automatically or with limited manual input.

Depending on the tool, it may monitor market conditions, generate signals, follow predefined rules, or execute trades through a supported platform. Some bots use visual interfaces that allow users to configure rules without programming. Others require code, technical configuration, or an API connection.

NO AUTOMATIC PROFITS

A trading bot is not automatically profitable. Its results depend on the strategy, market conditions, execution, costs, and how it is configured.

7 Things to Check Before Choosing a Trading Bot

1

Check Platform Compatibility

The first question is simple: does the bot support the trading platform and products you intend to use? Not every trading bot works with every broker, account, market, or trade type. A tool may support certain instruments but not others.

Before choosing a bot, check:

  • Which trading platforms it supports.
  • Which markets and products are available.
  • Whether an official integration or API is required.
  • Whether your intended account type is supported.
  • Whether the integration is currently maintained.

If you plan to use Deriv, review the official tools available through Deriv's trading platforms > and confirm third-party compatibility separately.

2

Understand How the Bot Makes Decisions

You should understand the rules that control the bot's behaviour. Some bots use predefined strategies. Others allow you to configure indicators, entry conditions, exit rules, or other parameters.

Before using one, ask yourself:

  • Can I understand the strategy it follows?
  • Can I change its settings?
  • Can I see why a trade or signal was generated?
  • Does the documentation explain its limitations?
  • Can I stop it when necessary?

Avoid relying on a tool that behaves like a black box if you cannot understand its risks or how to control it.

3

Look for Risk Management Controls

Risk management is one of the most important factors when evaluating trading automation. Depending on the tool, useful controls may include limits on stake size, maximum loss, trading duration, or the conditions under which the system stops.

However, these features are not available in every bot. Verify the actual controls instead of assuming they exist. Also remember that a stop condition cannot eliminate every possible risk (technical failures, execution slips, latency).

Important: Never choose a bot simply because it claims to recover losses or produce guaranteed profits. No legitimate trading strategy can guarantee a profitable result in every market condition.

4

Check Demo Testing and Backtesting Options

Testing helps you understand how a strategy behaves before you consider using real money:

  • Demo testing: Use a supported demo environment to observe the bot's behaviour with virtual funds.
  • Backtesting: Evaluate a strategy against historical data when the tool supports this functionality.

Both approaches have limitations. Historical data may not reflect future volatility, and demo trading may not reproduce every execution latency. Look beyond winning trades—review drawdowns and transaction costs.

5

Review Security and Account Permissions

A trading bot may require access to your trading account or permission to perform specific actions. The required access depends on how the tool integrates with the platform.

  • Check who operates the software.
  • Read the privacy policy and security documentation.
  • Understand every permission being requested.
  • Use official authentication or integration methods where available.
  • Never share your account password with an unverified service.
  • Avoid granting withdrawal permissions unless there is a clear, verified reason.
6

Compare Costs and Support

A trading bot may be free, subscription-based, or charge for particular features. There may also be separate trading costs from the broker or platform. Check the full cost rather than looking only at the advertised price:

  • Subscription or licence fees.
  • Additional charges for specific features or data relays.
  • Broker or trading commission costs.
  • Setup and maintenance requirements.
  • Documentation and customer support availability.

A low-cost bot is not necessarily the best choice, and an expensive bot is not necessarily more effective.

7

Check Transparency and Realistic Claims

Be cautious when a trading bot advertises guaranteed income, risk-free trading, or unusually high win rates without credible evidence.

Before deciding, look for clear documentation, realistic explanations of the strategy, honest risk disclosures, and information about the company or developer behind the tool. A trustworthy evaluation should explain both the tool's capabilities and its limitations.

Which Type of Trading Bot Is Suitable for Beginners?

The best starting point depends on what you want to learn and how much technical work you are comfortable with:

Trading Bot Selection Checklist

Before using any bot, make sure you can answer these essential questions:

Does it support my trading platform and intended product?

Do I understand the strategy and its settings?

Have I checked its risk management controls?

Can I test it in a demo sandbox before using real money?

Do I understand its account permissions and security?

Have I checked the total costs (licences + commissions)?

Are its claims realistic and transparent?

Do I know how to stop it and review its transaction activity?

If important questions remain unanswered, spend more time researching the tool before proceeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best trading bot for beginners?

There is no single best bot for everyone. Beginners should prioritise clear documentation, supported platform compatibility, understandable settings, appropriate risk controls, and opportunities to test a strategy.

Are free trading bots safe?

Not necessarily. A free tool can still have security issues, hidden limitations, or risky strategies. Check its developer, permissions, documentation, and reputation before using it.

Can a trading bot guarantee profits?

No. Automated trading involves uncertainty, and bots can make losing trades. Be sceptical of guaranteed-profit claims.

Can I use a trading bot with Deriv?

Deriv offers its own trading tools, including Deriv Bot. Third-party tools have their own compatibility requirements. Check the official platform documentation and the external tool's current supported integrations before using them.

Should I use a trading bot with real money immediately?

It is generally more prudent to understand the tool and test the strategy in a demo environment when available before considering real-money trading. Testing cannot guarantee future results.

Final Thoughts

Choosing a trading bot requires more than comparing prices or reading profit claims. You need to understand platform compatibility, strategy rules, risk controls, testing options, security, and ongoing costs.
