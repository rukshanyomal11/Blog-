When you open a trading terminal for the first time, you will immediately notice that every financial asset displays two distinct quotes: a buying price and a selling price.

The gap between these two numbers is universally known as the spread.

Understanding the spread is critical for every trader because it directly dictates the baseline cost of opening and closing positions. Even if your technical analysis predicts price direction accurately, accumulated spread friction can transform an apparently winning strategy into a net losing account.

In this beginner-friendly guide, you will learn what spread means, how to calculate it in points and pips, why spreads fluctuate dynamically, and what to verify before executing any trade.

What Is Spread in Trading?

The spread is the numerical difference between the Ask price (the rate at which you buy) and the Bid price (the rate at which you sell) of an asset.

Ask Price (Buy) 🟢 BUY

1.08250

The price at which you can instantly purchase an asset from the broker or market maker.

Bid Price (Sell) 🔴 SELL

1.08234

The price at which you can instantly sell an asset to the broker or market maker.

📐 The Basic Spread Formula
Spread = Ask Price − Bid Price

Example: 1.08250 − 1.08234 = 0.00016 (1.6 Pips on EUR/USD)

For standard Forex pairs (such as EUR/USD or GBP/USD), a "pip" represents the 4th decimal place (0.0001). In this example, the spread equals 1.6 pips. For JPY pairs, a pip corresponds to the 2nd decimal place (0.01). Always confirm your broker's pip conventions.

Why Does Spread Matter in Trading?

The spread is an upfront transaction fee automatically embedded into every execution. The instant you open a trade, your position begins with a small floating negative balance equal to the spread.

Worked Scenario: The Breakeven Hurdle

Suppose you buy an asset at an Ask price of $100.10 while the Bid price is $100.00. The spread is $0.10 per unit.

If you were to close the position immediately at the market Bid price, you would sell at $100.00, realizing a loss of $0.10 per unit.

This demonstrates why spread matters: the market must move in your direction by at least the width of the spread just for your position to break even before generating profit.

What Is the Difference Between Bid and Ask Prices?

The bid and ask prices represent the two opposing sides of a live two-way market quote:

Quote Element Hypothetical Quote Practical Meaning
Bid Price 1.1000 The highest price buyers/brokers currently offer to buy from you.
Ask Price 1.1002 The lowest price sellers/brokers currently accept to sell to you.
Raw Spread 0.0002 The arithmetic price gap (Ask − Bid).
Spread in Pips 2.0 Pips The standardized transaction cost for standard contracts.

What Are the Different Types of Spreads?

Brokers and trading platforms typically offer two primary pricing structures:

1. Fixed Spread

A fixed spread remains constant regardless of ordinary intraday market fluctuations. For instance, a broker might guarantee a 2.0-pip spread on EUR/USD throughout standard hours.

Advantage: High cost predictability for strategy modeling. However, fixed spreads are often slightly wider during quiet hours and may face execution slippage or requotes during extreme news shocks.

2. Variable (Floating) Spread

A variable spread fluctuates continuously according to interbank market conditions. During active trading sessions (such as the London/New York overlap), variable spreads can tighten to ultra-low levels (e.g., 0.2 to 0.6 pips). However, during volatile economic releases or illiquid market rollovers, variable spreads can suddenly widen to 5, 10, or 20 pips.

What Factors Affect the Spread?

Several fundamental and operational dynamics cause spreads to expand or contract:

  • 1. Market Liquidity: Highly traded major pairs (like EUR/USD) feature deep order books and razor-thin spreads. Exotic currency pairs or low-volume assets feature wide spreads due to scarce counterparties.
  • 2. Market Volatility: When prices move rapidly, liquidity providers widen their quotes to protect against sharp directional risk.
  • 3. Economic News and Releases: Major announcements like US Non-Farm Payrolls or central bank rate decisions cause spreads to balloon as institutional limit orders are temporarily withdrawn. Learn more in: How Does Economic News Affect Trading Markets?.
  • 4. Instrument Characteristics: Synthetic indices on platforms like Deriv have predetermined algorithmic spread characteristics that operate 24/7 without weekend closures.
  • 5. Broker Account Types: "Standard" accounts usually feature wider markups with zero commissions, while "Raw Spread" or "ECN" accounts feature tight 0.0-pip raw spreads paired with a separate commission fee per lot.

How Does Spread Affect Your Trading Results?

The total financial cost of a spread scales directly with your position size:

If you execute a trade of 1,000 units with an Ask of 10.02 and a Bid of 10.00, the immediate spread cost is: (10.02 − 10.00) × 1,000 = $20.00.

If you scale your position to 100,000 units (1 standard lot), that exact same 2-pip spread costs $200.00 on entry! Managing leverage and lot sizing is paramount to keeping spread overhead manageable.

Why Do Short-Term Traders Pay Attention to Spread?

High-frequency scalpers and short-term day traders are impacted far more severely by spreads than swing traders.

Consider the math: If a scalper on a 1-minute chart targets a quick gain of 5 pips, a 2-pip spread consumes a staggering 40% of their total potential profit! In contrast, for a swing trader targeting 150 pips on a 4-hour chart, a 2-pip spread represents just 1.3% of the target.

How to Check Spread Before Trading

Follow these 5 practical steps to evaluate spread conditions prior to entering an order:

Step 1: Select the Instrument

Open the market watch window on your platform and inspect the live Bid and Ask quotes.

Step 2: Calculate the Real-Time Difference

Subtract Bid from Ask to verify current spread in points or pips. Verify whether fractional pipettes (5th decimal place) are displayed.

Step 3: Monitor Spread Stability Over Time

Observe the spread across different hours. Spreads frequently widen significantly around the 5 PM EST daily rollover window.

Step 4: Check for Additional Commission Fees

Review whether your broker charges separate commissions per round turn, overnight swap financing rates, or account maintenance fees.

Step 5: Incorporate Spread Into Your Profit Target

Ensure your expected reward easily exceeds the spread cost. Follow our risk management frameworks in: How to Evaluate a Trading Strategy Before Using It.

Is a Lower Spread Always Better?

All else being equal, a smaller spread reduces friction. However, choosing a broker solely based on rock-bottom advertised spreads can be a trap.

You must evaluate total service quality:

  • Execution Quality: Does the broker execute market orders instantly, or do you suffer severe slippage? A 0.2-pip spread is useless if slippage costs you 2.0 pips.
  • Separate Commissions: Zero-spread accounts often charge $7 per lot commission, which is equivalent to a 0.7-pip spread cost.
  • Regulatory Security: Strong regulatory licensing, segregated client accounts, and fast withdrawal processing outweigh fractional pip differences.

How Does Spread Work With Automated Trading?

Algorithmic trading systems execute trades automatically based on technical rules (such as RSI oscillators or moving averages; see: What Is the RSI Indicator?). If an automated bot trades 20 times per day, spread costs accumulate rapidly.

Backtesting models that assume "zero spread" invariably fail when deployed on live markets. Automated strategies must always incorporate realistic spread buffers and slippage models. Learn more in: Can Trading Bots Lose Money?.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does spread mean in trading?

Spread is the difference between the ask price and the bid price of an asset. It is an important part of the cost of trading.

How do I calculate the spread?

Subtract the bid price from the ask price. For Forex, convert that price difference into pips according to the instrument's conventions.

Is spread a fee?

Spread is generally reflected in the difference between the buying and selling prices rather than being charged as a separate fee. Other fees or commissions may also apply.

Why does the spread increase?

Spreads may widen because of changing liquidity, volatility, market events, instrument characteristics, or provider pricing conditions.

What is a good spread for Forex?

There is no single spread that is good for every currency pair and trading situation. Compare the current spread with the instrument, provider terms, account type, and other trading costs.

Does spread affect automated trading bots?

Yes. Spread can affect the results of automated strategies, especially those that seek relatively small price movements or trade frequently.

Can I avoid spread completely?

In many products that quote separate bid and ask prices, the spread is an inherent part of the transaction. Some account structures may charge commissions alongside narrower spreads, so check the complete pricing model.

Conclusion

The spread is the fundamental gap between the buying (Ask) and selling (Bid) price of a financial asset. It represents an essential baseline transaction cost that must be overcome before any trade generates net profit.

By understanding how spreads are calculated, monitoring variable spread expansion around economic news, and accounting for transaction friction in your trading strategy, you can protect your trading capital from hidden fee erosion.
