When learning about online trading, you may come across two terms: Forex and synthetic indices.

Both can involve analysing price movements and making trading decisions. However, they are fundamentally different types of markets, and understanding how they work is important before choosing which one to study.

Forex is based on real-world currency exchange rates, while synthetic indices are designed to simulate certain market movements using computer-generated mathematical processes.

In this guide, we will explain the difference between Forex and synthetic indices, compare their main characteristics, and discuss the risks beginners should understand before risking capital.

What Is Forex Trading?

Forex, short for foreign exchange, is the global market where currencies are exchanged against one another.

Forex trading always involves currency pairs such as:

  • EUR/USD — Euro against the US dollar
  • GBP/USD — British pound against the US dollar
  • USD/JPY — US dollar against the Japanese yen

When trading a currency pair, you are speculating on how the value of one currency may change relative to the other. For example, if you trade EUR/USD, you are analysing the economic and monetary relationship between the European Union's euro and the United States dollar.

What affects Forex prices?

Forex prices are driven by complex real-world macroeconomic fundamentals:

  • Interest rate decisions by major central banks (Federal Reserve, ECB, Bank of England).
  • Inflation metrics (CPI, PPI) and employment data (Non-Farm Payrolls).
  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP) economic growth numbers.
  • Geopolitical developments, trade treaties, and sovereign events.
  • Broad market expectations and investor risk appetite.
  • Commercial supply and demand between transnational corporations and financial institutions.

Forex markets are constantly influenced by real-world events. Prices can experience high volatility within seconds when unexpected economic data or interest rate statements are released.

What Are Synthetic Indices?

Synthetic indices are financial instruments designed to simulate specific market behaviours. On platforms that offer them, their prices are generated using computer-based algorithms rather than being directly linked to the physical exchange rate of a sovereign currency pair.

Deriv is one well-known trading platform widely recognized for pioneering and offering synthetic indices (such as Volatility Indices, Crash/Boom, and Step Indices).

Depending on the instrument, synthetic indices may be designed to exhibit consistent mathematical characteristics, such as constant constant-volatility percentages (e.g., Volatility 75 or Volatility 25) or sudden, periodic price spikes and drops (e.g., Crash 500 or Boom 1000).

The exact price behavior and rules depend on the specific index and the provider's product documentation.

How do synthetic indices work?

Unlike Forex currency pairs, synthetic indices are not based on the relative valuation of two national economies. Instead, their prices are generated algorithmically according to the broker provider's cryptographically verified mathematical systems.

Traders can still inspect visual candlestick charts, draw trendlines, and use technical indicators to structure trade ideas. However, they must understand that simulated market behavior operates independently of real-world economic releases.

Forex vs Synthetic Indices: Main Differences

The following table summarizes the primary mechanical and operational distinctions between the two markets:

What is traded? Sovereign currency pairs (e.g., EUR/USD, GBP/JPY) Provider-generated algorithmic index contracts
Price drivers Macroeconomic data, interest rates, capital flows, market sentiment Computer-generated processes defined by the platform provider
Economic news influence Can be significant; high-impact news causes violent slippage and repricing Not directly driven by ordinary real-world economic news events

1. How Price Movements Are Different

One of the central differences between the two markets is what causes prices to fluctuate.

Forex Price Movements

Forex quotes adjust to real-time changes in economic sentiment. For instance, if unexpected inflation data pushes expectations toward higher US interest rates, institutional investors may buy the dollar, shifting EUR/USD lower.

However, currency markets are complex—multiple macroeconomic factors pull in opposite directions simultaneously, creating erratic market swings.

Synthetic Index Price Movements

Synthetic indices rely on algorithmic formulas calibrated to maintain fixed volatility ranges. They are unaffected by GDP prints, political elections, or corporate earnings.

Some indices simulate continuous smooth volatility, while others feature programmed step movements or sudden drops. Past chart patterns never guarantee future outcomes.

2. Do Forex and Synthetic Indices Trade at the Same Times?

No. Market availability is distinctly different between the two categories.

The global Forex market operates roughly 24 hours a day, five days a week. Trading commences in Sydney and Tokyo, shifts to London and Frankfurt, and concludes each Friday evening in New York. On weekends, interbank currency markets close.

In contrast, synthetic indices offered by providers such as Deriv run on server algorithms and are generally accessible 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. They do not close over weekends or international bank holidays (subject to occasional scheduled maintenance).

Trading Caution: Just because an instrument is accessible around the clock does not mean it is appropriate to trade at all hours. Fatigue and continuous exposure to risk can degrade trading discipline.

3. Which Market Is Easier for Beginners?

There is no universal or straightforward answer to this question.

  • Forex may appeal to beginners who enjoy following international news, studying currency economics, and understanding global monetary policy.
  • Synthetic indices may appeal to individuals who prefer trading without the unpredictability of unexpected economic news headlines, or who need to trade during weekend hours when conventional markets are closed.

However, neither market is inherently easy or safe. Before committing capital to either market, beginners should first learn:

  1. Exactly how the underlying contract is calculated and executed.
  2. How bid and ask prices are quoted and how spreads affect profitability.
  3. How a trade produces a loss under adverse price movement.
  4. Whether leverage or contract multipliers amplify potential losses.
  5. How to practice using a free demo account before risking real funds.

4. Is Technical Analysis the Same for Both?

Many classic technical analysis concepts can be applied when analyzing both Forex charts and synthetic indices:

  • Candlestick patterns: Identifying engulfing candles, hammers, and pin bars (see our tutorial on How to Read Candlestick Charts).
  • Support and resistance: Marking key horizontal pivot zones and swing highs/lows.
  • Trends and ranges: Identifying trendlines, channels, and consolidation phases.
  • Mathematical indicators: Moving averages, Bollinger Bands, and momentum oscillators like the Relative Strength Index (RSI).

However, applying the same indicator does not mean the markets will react identically. A moving average crossover strategy backtested on EUR/USD might produce completely different results on a high-frequency Volatility 100 Index. Each market's underlying volatility distribution, tick frequency, and slippage characteristics must be tested independently.

5. Which Market Is More Risky?

It is not accurate to label one market as categorically riskier than the other. Risk is primarily determined by position sizing, leverage, market conditions, and a trader's execution discipline.

Forex risks include sharp volatility during central bank announcements, unexpected liquidity crunches during session overlaps, and weekend price gaps that bypass stop-loss orders.

Synthetic index risks include high constant volatility, rapid tick fluctuations, and specific product mechanics where losses can accumulate swiftly if trades are unmonitored.

Key Risk Checklist:
  • Understand the contract specification and maximum possible drawdown before opening an order.
  • Always enforce defined risk limits and never risk more than 1–2% of account equity on a single position.
  • Master demo accounts thoroughly before committing actual capital.
  • Never trade with borrowed funds or essential living expenses.

6. Can You Use Automated Trading for Both?

Yes, algorithmic trading software can be deployed on both markets, but the technical execution environment differs substantially.

Forex bots typically connect via MetaTrader (MT4/MT5) expert advisors (EAs) or FIX API endpoints, processing order books and managing spreads across multiple liquidity providers.

Synthetic index bots typically connect directly to specialized broker APIs, such as Deriv's WebSocket API, executing tick-based trades or binary/multiplier contracts according to predetermined mathematical parameters.

7. How to Choose Between Forex and Synthetic Indices

Use the following criteria to evaluate which market aligns better with your educational background and personal goals:

Choose Forex for Further Study If:

  • You have a keen interest in international economics and currency markets.
  • You want to learn how global central bank policies influence trade flows.
  • You prefer trading standard weekday sessions and taking weekends off.
  • You plan to use institutional fundamental data alongside chart technicals.

Explore Synthetic Indices If:

  • You want to understand algorithmic, computer-generated price mechanics.
  • You require access to weekend trading hours due to work schedules.
  • You prefer markets free from unpredictable geopolitical news releases.
  • You are willing to study provider-specific volatility metrics and contract rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are synthetic indices the same as Forex?

No. Forex involves the trading of physical sovereign currency pairs driven by real-world economies, whereas synthetic indices are designed to simulate market behavior using computer-generated mathematical processes defined by the platform provider.

Are synthetic indices affected by economic news?

They are not directly driven by ordinary macroeconomic news announcements (such as interest rate cuts or inflation reports) in the same way as Forex currency pairs. However, traders should always check the provider's official documentation for specific instrument dynamics.

Can I trade synthetic indices on Deriv?

Deriv is widely known for pioneering synthetic indices. Availability, contract specifications, and regional access may vary, so verify details on the official platform at Deriv Trading Platforms.

Can I use the same strategy for Forex and synthetic indices?

While you can use common technical analysis concepts (such as support/resistance and candlestick patterns) across both, you must test the strategy separately on each instrument. Different market structures and volatility profiles will yield very different performance results.

Which is more profitable: Forex or synthetic indices?

Neither market is guaranteed to be more profitable. Profitability depends entirely on your strategy's edge, risk management, transaction fees, execution speed, and emotional discipline. Substantial losses are possible in both markets.

Final Thoughts

Forex and synthetic indices are distinctly different types of trading markets. Forex currency prices reflect the real-time monetary relationships between nations, while synthetic indices generate simulated volatility through proprietary mathematical algorithms.

Both environments demand disciplined risk management, solid education, and extensive paper testing. Before selecting an instrument, understand its underlying mechanics, test your setup thoroughly in a demo account, and never assume that technical charts or automated bots guarantee success.
