What Is Drawdown in Trading? A Beginner's Guide

TI
Trade Insight Quantitative Research Published October 12, 2026 • 9 min read
Drawdown quantifies the historical percentage decline from an account's highest equity peak to its subsequent trough, measuring the psychological and financial depth of losing streaks.
Trading is rarely a smooth, straight-line journey upward. An account will expand during favorable market cycles, experience inevitable losing streaks, and either recover to new peaks—or suffer severe capital erosion.

When assessing any trading strategy or automated bot, evaluating profit potential alone paints a dangerously incomplete picture. The true measure of a strategy's durability is revealed by its drawdown.

Drawdown measures the distance an account balance falls from its highest previous peak to a subsequent low. Quantitative traders and hedge fund managers examine drawdown to understand the downside volatility and emotional pressure required to trade a system.

In this beginner's guide, you will learn what drawdown means, master the step-by-step formulas, understand the critical asymmetry of recovery math, and discover why tracking drawdown is vital in automated trading.

What Is Drawdown in Trading?

Drawdown is the measure of a trading account or portfolio's decline in value from a historical high point (peak) to a subsequent low point (trough) before a new high is achieved.

A Simple Illustration

Imagine your trading account grows from $1,000 to an equity peak of $1,200. Subsequently, during an adverse market regime, your account balance falls to $1,080.

Your account has declined by $120 from its peak. This $120 decline represents your monetary drawdown. Expressed as a percentage, your drawdown is ($120 ÷ $1,200) × 100 = 10%.

How to Calculate Drawdown

Drawdown is calculated using two simple equations:

Formula 1: Monetary Drawdown Amount
Drawdown Amount ($) = Previous Peak Value − Trough Value
Formula 2: Percentage Drawdown (%)
Drawdown (%) = (Drawdown Amount ÷ Previous Peak Value) × 100

Worked Example

Suppose a trading account achieves a record peak balance of $2,000. Over the following two weeks, a string of losses reduces the account to $1,700 before turning back upward.

  • Step 1 (Decline Amount): $2,000 − $1,700 = $300
  • Step 2 (Decline Fraction): $300 ÷ $2,000 = 0.15
  • Step 3 (Percentage Conversion): 0.15 × 100 = 15%

The strategy experienced a 15% drawdown from that specific peak. Only when account equity breaks above $2,000 is the drawdown officially considered fully recovered.

What Is Maximum Drawdown (MDD)?

Maximum Drawdown (MDD) is the single largest peak-to-trough percentage decline recorded across an entire testing period or trading history.

Consider an account moving through three distinct cycles:

Cycle Phase Equity Peak Subsequent Trough Observed Drawdown
Decline #1 $1,000 $900 10%
Decline #2 $1,100 $880 20% (Max)
Decline #3 $1,200 $1,080 10%

In this trading history, the Maximum Drawdown is 20%. MDD provides the ultimate stress test metric: it tells you the worst historical drawdown an investor or automated strategy had to endure.

Vital Reality: Historical maximum drawdown is not a guarantee of future limits. Changing market volatility or slippage can easily create a new record maximum drawdown in live trading.

What Is the Difference Between Drawdown and a Losing Trade?

A losing trade is an isolated transaction that closes with negative profit. It is a single data point.

In contrast, drawdown measures the cumulative portfolio contraction caused by a series of consecutive losses, trading fees, commissions, and spread costs over time.

A trader risking 1% per trade might view single losses as trivial. However, six consecutive losses followed by choppy break-even trades can create an accumulated 7% to 8% drawdown from the peak. Evaluating drawdown provides a holistic view of systemic risk.

Balance Drawdown vs Equity Drawdown

Most trading terminals report both Balance and Equity. Understanding which metric is used in your drawdown calculation is essential:

  • Balance Drawdown: Calculated exclusively from realized, closed trades. Ignores floating open losses.
  • Equity Drawdown (Floating Drawdown): Incorporates the real-time floating profit and loss of currently open positions.
Why Equity Drawdown Matters: A trader or grid bot might hold ten open losing trades down -$500 without closing them, claiming "zero balance drawdown." However, real account equity has plunged. Always demand Equity Drawdown when evaluating trading strategies and automated bots.

The Mathematics of Drawdown Recovery: An Unforgiving Asymmetry

One of the most dangerous mathematical traps for beginners is failing to realize that the percentage gain required to recover from a drawdown is always larger than the percentage lost.

If you have $1,000 and lose 50% ($500), you are left with $500. To get back to your original $1,000, you must generate a 100% gain on your remaining capital!

Account Drawdown (%) Remaining Account Equity Gain Required to Break Even Recovery Difficulty Level
10% 90% ($900) +11.11% Manageable with disciplined trading
20% 80% ($800) +25.00% Moderate; requires patient execution
30% 70% ($700) +42.86% Challenging; high psychological friction
50% 50% ($500) +100.00% Extreme; doubling an account is difficult
75% 25% ($250) +300.00% Catastrophic; rarely recovered

This stark mathematical reality illustrates why strict capital protection and position sizing are non-negotiable: keeping drawdowns shallow (under 15–20%) keeps recovery within realistic reach.

What Causes Large Drawdowns?

Severe drawdowns rarely happen by bad luck alone. They are typically driven by identifiable risk errors:

1. Oversized Position Sizing

Risking 5% or 10% per trade means a standard four-trade losing streak triggers an instant 20–40% drawdown. Master proper calculations in: How to Calculate Position Size.

2. Martingale Recovery Trading

Doubling trade volume after each loss to "recoup quickly." When an extended streak occurs, martingale strategies suffer exponential account liquidation.

3. Shifting Market Regimes

A trend-following system deployed during a choppy sideways market will get whipsawed repeatedly. Review: Why Do Market Prices Go Up and Down?.

4. Unchecked High Leverage

High leverage magnifies position exposure relative to margin equity, accelerating account liquidation during sudden volatility spikes.

How Can Traders Manage and Limit Drawdown?

While drawdown can never be eliminated entirely, disciplined risk controls ensure drawdowns remain survivable:

  1. Enforce a 1% Rule: Cap risk per trade strictly between 1% and 2% of total equity.
  2. Set a Maximum Daily / Weekly Loss Limit: If your account experiences a 5% decline in a single week, halt trading immediately and review your strategy.
  3. Maintain a Detailed Journal: Track your equity curve and record peak-to-trough fluctuations. Follow: How to Keep a Trading Journal: A Beginner's Guide.
  4. Avoid Revenge Trading: Never increase lot sizes during a drawdown. Lower position size instead to preserve emotional discipline.
  5. Backtest on Out-of-Sample Data: Validate your strategy's historical drawdowns across multiple market conditions. Review: How to Evaluate a Trading Strategy Before Using It.

Why Is Drawdown Important in Automated Trading?

In algorithmic trading, bots execute trades without human emotion. However, an automated bot will continue opening trades relentlessly during an adverse market regime unless programmed with strict drawdown-protection circuits.

When analyzing automated trading bots, always examine the Return-to-Drawdown ratio (e.g., Calmar ratio). A bot advertising 50% annual returns with an 80% maximum drawdown is a ticking time bomb, whereas a bot producing 25% returns with an 8% drawdown represents an institutional-grade system. Frequently Asked Questions

What does drawdown mean in simple terms?

Drawdown is how far an account's value falls from a previous peak to a later low point.

How is drawdown calculated?

Subtract the lower value from the previous peak to find the monetary decline. Divide that decline by the peak value and multiply by 100 to calculate the percentage.

What is maximum drawdown?

Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline recorded during a specified period.

Is a 20% drawdown bad?

A 20% drawdown means an account declined by 20% from its relevant previous peak. Whether that is acceptable depends on the strategy, financial circumstances, and risk plan. A larger drawdown also requires a larger percentage gain to recover.

Can a trading strategy have profits and high drawdown?

Yes. A strategy may finish a period with positive returns while experiencing a substantial decline along the way. Returns and drawdown should be evaluated together.

Can automated trading eliminate drawdown?

No. Automated trading can follow predefined rules, but it cannot eliminate market risk or guarantee that losses will remain within a particular limit.

Does drawdown include open trades?

It depends on the measurement method. Equity-based drawdown can reflect unrealised gains and losses from open positions, while balance-based drawdown may reflect only recorded account balance changes.

Conclusion

Drawdown is the ultimate health metric of any trading career. Understanding peak-to-trough contractions, respecting the asymmetrical math of recovery, and keeping drawdowns controlled through disciplined position sizing ensures that your portfolio survives market storms.

Never judge a trader or automated bot by their best winning days. Judge them by how gracefully they protect capital during their deepest drawdowns.
